Fishing trip closeout checklist for fleet and processor teams

A vessel returning to port can leave three different teams with three different versions of the trip: field records waiting to sync, a coordinator's trip timeline, and a processor's lot record. Use this checklist to agree which record is ready for handoff and who owns the remaining gaps.

Download the editable closeout checklist (CSV)

Who should review the landing?

The captain or field operator confirms the device records. The fleet coordinator reconciles the trip and outstanding actions. The processor checks the lot and its source references. Assign a named reviewer for each step before the vessel returns; these roles are an evaluation workflow, not a prescribed staffing model.

When is a trip ready for review?

An empty upload queue alone does not prove that all expected work reached the shore team. Compare the devices used, accepted events, rejected uploads, and the latest trip state. Keep unresolved records visible and assign follow-up before using the shared timeline as the basis for a lot handoff.

Try one representative trip before a fleet rollout

  1. Choose a vessel, field device, coordinator, and processor reviewer. Record the identifiers they will use.
  2. Prepare access while connected, then record representative work with connectivity disabled.
  3. Reconnect and inspect accepted and rejected events in the shared record.
  4. Introduce a missing field or receiving discrepancy and confirm that the reviewer can identify its owner and supporting record.
  5. Complete the lot review and verify the resulting certificate. Compare the result with the processor's actual receiving and onward-traceability needs.

How does Northline support the review?

Northline provides offline field queues, sync health, trip timelines, lot records, closeout review, and certificate verification. Its mobile app supports field activity, its tablet app supports vessel handoffs, and its portal supports shore-side review. Start with the offline operations guide and lot traceability workflow to map those surfaces to your team.

What should a buyer verify separately?

Confirm the reporting system and integrations required by your fishery and customers. DFO maintains a qualification process for electronic logbook applications; this guide does not establish Northline as a qualified ELOG submission product. A Northline lot certificate records operational provenance and should not be treated as regulatory approval or a seafood sustainability certification.

CFIA's traceability guidance includes identifying food and its source and destination. Check how your receiving, processing, and shipping records supply that wider chain; a trip-to-lot link covers only part of it.
