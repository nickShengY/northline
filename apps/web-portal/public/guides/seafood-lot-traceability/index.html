Seafood lot traceability and trip closeout

Northline connects a seafood lot with its trip and the events used to assemble its provenance record. Fleet and processor teams can review lots, issue lot certificates, and verify a certificate record from the web portal.

What connects a lot to its source?

A Northline lot certificate includes the lot ID, trip ID, vessel or group, issuing actor, issue time, and provenance event IDs. These identifiers connect the certificate to the operational timeline that supplied its context.

For a processor reviewing a landing, the useful starting point is the lot itself: which trip it belongs to, which source events describe it, and which certificate was issued for that record.

From trip review to certificate

  1. Inspect the trip timeline and confirm that the field records have synchronized.
  2. Review the lot and the provenance events attached to it.
  3. Resolve missing closeout fields and review the compliance status.
  4. Issue the certificate and retain its ID with the lot record.
  5. Use certificate verification when checking the record again.

How is the certificate record structured?

The certificate payload is stored as a JSON artifact with a SHA-256 digest and associated metadata. Its provenance event IDs identify the timeline records used to create the certificate context. This structure lets the record carry its source references forward through a handoff.

What should teams check before closeout?

Review the completion meter, errors, warnings, required signatures, and audit history together. A complete record depends on the underlying trip information, so an unresolved field upload belongs in the closeout review as well as in the sync monitor.

Who uses the traceability workflow?

Vessel teams supply trip and field activity. Fleet coordinators review the operational history. Processor teams work with lots and certificate records. A shared set of identifiers helps those roles discuss the same landing without rebuilding the context from separate messages.
